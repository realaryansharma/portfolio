Nothing Is Random

Your coin flip, your dice, your lottery ticket. "Random" is just a word for what we can't keep track of.

Nothing is random. Not your coin flip. Not your dice. Not your lottery ticket. "Random" is just the word we use for what we cannot keep track of.

The coin that was never random

Flip a coin. If you knew the exact force of your thumb, the angle, the air, and the surface it lands on, you would know how it lands before it lands. Researchers have even built coin-flipping machines that land the same side every time.

The coin was never random. You just cannot see everything.

Computers cannot even pretend

Now computers. They cannot pretend to not know. You give them instructions, and the same input produces the same output, every single time. That is exactly why we trust them.

But randomness is the one thing you cannot calculate. Because if you can calculate it, it was never random.

How a machine fakes it

So how does a computer fake it? It grabs something humans cannot track. The exact time, down to the nanosecond. The tiny timing jitter inside your processor. It mixes those together, throws them into a formula, and out comes something that looks random.

Underneath, there is always a pattern. If you have written any Java, you have met this. Create a java.util.Random with the same seed and you get the same "random" numbers on every run. It is a very convincing flip book, not a dice roll.

"'Random' is just a word for what we can't keep track of."

Borrowing chaos from the outside world

For passwords and encryption, a flip book is not enough. So the machine looks outside itself. The microseconds between your keystrokes. The tiny wiggle of your mouse. Electrical noise humming inside the chip. Chaos it cannot predict, so neither can an attacker.

In Java, SecureRandom exists for exactly this reason. It leans on the operating system's pool of unpredictable events instead of a tidy formula.

And my favourite example: Cloudflare keeps a wall of lava lamps in its San Francisco lobby. A camera films the wax as it floats, and that footage helps secure parts of the internet.

Randomness, borrowed from our ignorance

But even the lava follows physics. It is just too complex for anyone to follow. So the most precise machine we have ever built borrows its randomness from our ignorance.

Which makes me wonder: is randomness nothing but the inability of the human mind to understand what is going on? And if nothing is truly random, are your choices? I chased that edge in The Robot Who Couldn't Wake Up, and I still do not have a clean answer.

Margin note

Same seed, same numbers, every run. Try it in Java and watch the "random" repeat.
