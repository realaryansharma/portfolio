Where Did the Year Go?

Time did not speed up. You just stopped giving it anything to remember.

Remember when summer holidays felt endless? Six weeks that felt like a small lifetime. And now a whole year disappears in a blink, usually somewhere between a Monday standup and a Friday deploy.

Here is the uncomfortable part. Time did not speed up. The clock is exactly as slow as it was when you were ten. What changed is what you give it to remember.

Your brain does not have a clock

It has a memory. When you look back and ask yourself, "how long was that?", your brain does not check a timer. It counts the moments it saved. More memories, and the stretch feels long. Fewer memories, and it feels like it barely happened.

So there are really two kinds of time. The time you live through, and the time you remember having lived through. We keep confusing the two, and then wonder why the calendar feels rigged.

The engineer in me sees compression

I write software for a living, so let me explain this the way I would explain a storage problem. Your brain is a compression algorithm. Same commute. Same desk. Same scroll. Same meal. Day after day, identical data.

And any good system, when handed duplicates, deletes them. Monday through Friday gets zipped into one file named "Work". Five days, saved as one. Multiply that by fifty weeks and you get a year that fits in a thumbnail.

"Time did not speed up. You just stopped giving it anything to remember."

The holiday paradox

Now take a trip somewhere new. New streets, new food, new faces, new mistakes. Every hour is new data, and there is nothing to compress. That is why a holiday flies by while you are living it, but when you look back, that one week feels longer than the last three months.

Psychologists call it the holiday paradox. The present moment and the remembered moment are keeping two different sets of books.

So is life short?

Maybe life is not short. Maybe it is just repetitive. You cannot add more days to your life, but you can add more life to your days.

Learn something. Go somewhere. Talk to someone new. Take a different route to work, which is the cheapest holiday you can buy. Give your brain something worth saving.

Complex things are just simple things, stacked. Even a feeling as big as "where did the year go?" turns out to be a storage policy.
