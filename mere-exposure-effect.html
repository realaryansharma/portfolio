The Mentalist and the Interview

How a mentalist guesses your number, why you dislike your own photos, and what the mere-exposure effect does to your interview odds.

Ever seen a mentalist guess what you are thinking? "Think of a number," they say. You pick six, and they knew it the whole time.

Rewind, and look closer. Six buttons on his shirt. Three fingers up on each hand, which adds up to six. Six people standing behind him. Nothing random. All planned. You never noticed any of it. Your brain did.

Your brain sees everything

Even right now, your eyes take in far more than you focus on. You are reading this, but your eyes are also catching whatever is happening around your phone or screen. All of it goes into your mind, and you ignore almost all of it.

Until a moth flies past at the corner of your eye, and you look before you even decide to. Because your brain was watching that space the whole time.

The hack

Here is where it gets weird. Whatever your brain sees again and again, it starts to like. Even if you never consciously noticed it. It is called the mere-exposure effect.

"Familiar feels easy. Easy feels safe. And safe feels right."

Aryan Sharma

The photo and the mirror

Do not believe me? Ever hated a photo of yourself while your reflection in the mirror looks fine? Your friends feel the exact opposite. You see your mirror image every day, and they see the real you. Same face, different familiarity.

The real-world version

Now flip the script. You have an interview, and you know who is on the other side. Before the day, you visit their profile. You follow them. You leave a thoughtful comment, not just a like. You post about the things they care about, and you read what they write.

They barely notice. A name, a face, one glance. That is it. But on interview day, you are not a stranger. You have already been in their head.

Now assume three candidates. Same skills. Same stance. So whose name will the interviewer feel good about? Who gets the chance?

One honest warning. This only works when the interest is real. Fake engagement reads as fake, and repeated noise turns familiarity into irritation. Read their work, then say something worth reading.

It will feel like luck

It will feel like luck. It is not. And the slightly creepy part is that this is being done to you every single day, by ads, feeds, and brands that would very much like to be familiar.

Open your eyes, observe, and learn by watching yourself.

Margin note

Familiarity is the oldest growth hack there is. Notice what keeps showing up in your feed, and ask who put it there.
