DISPATCH #01 · PHILOSOPHY 4 MIN READ

A Beautiful Room

On hedonic adaptation, aesthetic permanence, and internal architecture.

Have you ever felt stuck in your life? You search for clarity, find an answer that offers temporary relief, but even after understanding it, the pattern repeats. It is perpetual.

Let’s take an analogy to understand this: A Beautiful Room.

Imagine you bought a new room. You designed it exactly the way you wanted: all great supporting colors, action figures, paintings, or whatever happens to appeal to you.

The room feels premium. You love the vibe. You invite your friends over for a party; everyone heaps praise on the space, and you feel amazing.

The party ends. A few days, then a few weeks pass. The room gradually starts feeling normal, average, and devoid of that initial surge. Dirt quietly accumulates in the corners. You don’t enjoy living there the same way anymore.

So, what do you do? You decide to make the room beautiful again. You purchase new decorative pieces, repaint with fresher colors, and string up warmer lights.

You feel good again. You party with friends; everyone praises the setup once more.

Yet again—a few weeks pass, and you are right back to square one: the dull room.

"To make the room beautiful, you never had to change the room. You need to understand what is beauty to you that never dulls."

— Aryan Sharma

The loop never breaks by continually remodeling external wallpaper. It only breaks when you stop treating surface novelty as lasting peace.

Margin note

I wrote this after redecorating my apartment for the third time in a year. The room was never the problem.
